How the numbers are made

Where the data comes from

F&O turnover. A relay reads MCX’s market data every 15 minutes during the session and stores a snapshot: futures notional, option premium and the most active contracts. After the close it writes the day’s final record, and the next morning’s job checks it against MCX’s bhavcopy.

Share price. The NSE close for each day, and during NSE hours a live price (indianapi.in, falling back to Yahoo) refreshed every minute on Fair value and Scenarios.

Reported results. Quarterly revenue, expenses and profit are entered from MCX’s filings when each quarter is reported.

Signals. After each close the jobs recompute fair value, then the price-gap and multi-factor models, then momentum.

Participants and margins. MCX’s disclosures, collected by scheduled jobs; each page shows the date of the data it uses.

Revenue from turnover

MCX charges a fee on each side of every trade, at SEBI’s flat rates since October 2024:

futures revenue = 2 × futures notional × ₹210 per crore
options revenue = 2 × option premium  × ₹4,180 per crore

These are transaction fees only. MCX’s reported revenue also includes other operating income (lately about 9% of F&O fees) and other income, which the daily figures leave out; Quarter P&L can add an estimate back.

The live projection

Futures and options are projected separately: what has been booked so far, multiplied up to a full day.

multiplier = w × (1 ÷ share of hours gone) + (1 − w) × (1 ÷ share of a normal day done)
w = (share of hours gone)^1.5

The share of a normal day done by now comes from an intraday curve, a weighted average of recent complete days (half-life 10 trading days). Early in the day the curve dominates; by the evening straight-line extrapolation takes over.

A drift check compares today’s completed time buckets with that curve and scales the curve’s multiplier, capped at ±25%. On live data it sits at the +25% cap on most days from mid-morning, which offsets the curve’s tendency to project low; it stays until a calibration by time of day replaces it. A prior for scheduled events fades to nothing by the close.

The likely range

Measured, not modelled. Every live projection stored over the last 90 normal trading days is compared with that day’s final revenue:

error = projection ÷ final − 1
likely range = projection ÷ (1 + q90)  to  projection ÷ (1 + q10)

q10 and q90 are the 10th and 90th percentile errors for projections made at the same half hour, so on 8 days in 10 the final landed inside the range. Because the error is measured against the final, the range reaches further up than down.

Part-day sessions and US market holidays are left out, and there is no range before 09:30. The same figures drive the chart “How far to trust the projection” and Lab › Diagnostics.

Completed days and averages

A day counts only once it is complete: a day before today with a stored record, or today after the close once its final record has landed (the relay’s end-of-day row or MCX’s historical file). The live day never enters an average.

The windows are the last 5, 10, 20 and 45 trading days, and the quarter and financial year (April to March) so far. Each short window is compared with the same number of days just before it; the quarter and year with the whole previous quarter and year, and the year also with the same stretch of last year.

Quarter P&L

Revenue: F&O revenue booked so far, plus a daily projection for each session left. Today counts as left until its final record lands. The daily projection blends the last 10 sessions with the quarter’s average so far, leaning on the last 10 as the quarter goes on (at most 80%).

expenses = α + β × revenue   (at least ₹80 Cr; + ₹15 Cr in Q4)
profit   = (revenue − expenses) × (1 − tax-and-depreciation rate)

α and β are fitted to reported quarters; the tax-and-depreciation rate is the median of past quarters.

Two bases. F&O only, the default, or including non-F&O revenue, estimated at the previous quarter’s share of F&O revenue (used only when that share is between 0 and 20% and the quarter’s daily records are nearly complete). A walk-forward test on the last five reported quarters shows how each basis has missed.

Fair value

Tusk house view, following the Tusk sheet:

FY28 revenue = ₹15.00 × 260 + non-F&O ₹211 Cr × 1.2 × 1.15 + other income ₹127 Cr × 1.2 × 1.15
FY28 EPS     = FY28 revenue × 57% ÷ 25.451 Cr shares
today        = P/E × FY28 EPS ÷ 1.18 ÷ 1.09     (P/E 42, 48, 54)

Every input can be changed on the page, and the second discount can switch to pro-rata: 18% × the days left to 31 March 2027.

Data-driven view, the stock’s own valuation history applied to today’s earnings:

run-rate EPS = (45-day revenue per day × 256 + ₹374.5 Cr) × 55% ÷ shares
fair value   = run-rate EPS × median P/E of the last 252 days

The band is the median ± a robust standard deviation (1.4826 × the median absolute deviation). There is no discounting. The regression of price on the 45-day average and the six brokers’ targets are shown as cross-checks only.

Momentum

Regime: the 10-day average revenue divided by the 45-day. Above 1.05 is hot, below 0.95 cold, in between neutral.

Price overlay: the share price’s move over 5 days and its average daily move (close to close) over 5 days against 20. Breakout: up more than 1.5% with the 5-day move above 1.5× the 20-day. Quiet rise: up more than 1% with it below 0.8×. Sharp fall: down more than 1% with it above 1.3×.

Composite: hot reads strong buy unless the overlay is a sharp fall (buy); neutral reads buy on a breakout or quiet rise, otherwise hold; cold reads sell, or watch after a sharp fall.

Model ensemble

Price gap (error correction): the share price against the data-driven fair value, in %, as a z-score against its last 60 days (at least 30).

Exchange activity (multi-factor): daily revenue and turnover, each as a z-score against its last 60 days, blended 3 : 4. Volume and intraday range are computed but not used.

score = 0.30 × (−price-gap z) + 0.70 × (3/7 × revenue z + 4/7 × turnover z)
      = 30% price gap + 30% revenue + 40% turnover

Above +0.5 reads buy and above +1.5 strong buy, mirrored for sell. The implied position is the score ÷ 2, capped at ±1.

How signals are tested

A day’s signal uses that day’s MCX revenue, which is known only after the evening session ends at 23:30, hours after the NSE close. So every test starts at the next day’s close, the first chance to act.

Rolling windows use only returns already known on their date. Forward windows overlap, so day counts overstate the independent evidence, and the rules were chosen on the same history, which flatters them. Each result is shown beside the figure for all days.

Data source settings

The relay pushes MCX data every 15 minutes, so this is rarely needed. A manual refresh asks the server to fetch from MCX directly; MCX usually blocks cloud servers, and a browser session cookie can get past that. It stays in this browser.
